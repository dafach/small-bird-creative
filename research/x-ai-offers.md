## How to read the catalog

Each offer below is something a real person or firm is selling in public. Education products (Maker School, Vibe Marketers, AAA Hub) are included because they are the *actual* offers those X operators sell most loudly, even when they also have a client-services shop.

Price bands that show up over and over:

| Rung | Typical public range (2026) | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Free qualify | $0 · 15–30 min | Funnel map, readiness scorecard, “are we a fit” |
| Paid hour / office hour | $300–$1,000 | Working session, not a sales call |
| Productized assessment | $750–$7,000 (SMB) · $15k+ (mid-market) | Written diagnosis + roadmap |
| One-workflow build | $1,000–$40,000 | Fixed-scope ship |
| Done-with-you month | $1,200–$3,500/mo | Two calls + async; client steers |
| Done-for-you / managed | $3,500–$15,000+/mo | They run it |
| Fractional CAIO | $5,000–$30,000/mo | Executive ownership, not implementation |

Small Bird’s $300 hour sits at the cheap, honest end of “paid hour.” The $999 / $1.2–2k band that Adam is exploring sits dead-center in the SMB productized-assessment + DWY-month market — which is also exactly where Corey has been teaching the playbook in public. That is the tension for the morning page work.

### 1. Corey Ganim / Return My Time — the playbook everyone is copying

- **Who:** Corey Ganim. Co-founder, Return My Time (with technical partner Nick; Nick was a software developer at Vanguard 15 years). Charlotte, NC. Newsletter: Corey's Notes (Kit), 28k+ readers. Podcast: Build With AI. Previously built a ~$16M e-com business, shut it in Oct 2025.
- **Handle if public:** Posts as Corey Ganim on LinkedIn and X. Site does not list a handle.
- **ICP he states in the Vibe Marketers session:** operators, $1–10M revenue, 3–20 employees, buried in manual work. Knowledge-base / “second brain” work is a different ICP: $3–50M+ (he cited a multi-billion public company and a $150M company for KB assessments).
- **Principle he repeats everywhere:** clients buy outcomes (effectiveness / efficiency / quality), not “AI.” “Assessment” not “audit” (audit sounds punitive).

#### 1a. AI Tools Assessment — $999, productized one-off

- **Offer name:** AI Tools Assessment (he also says “AI assessment”; he avoids “audit”).
- **Price / format:** **$999** one-off. First 2–3 free for testimonials, then $200 (clients devalued it), then $500, then $1,000 as the sweet spot. In the Vibe session he says he has sold ~15 this year; ~3 from social, the rest from warm texts.
- **What the buyer gets (4 steps, ~2 hours of Corey):**
  1. 45-min discovery. Core question: “Walk me through yesterday. Where did your time go?” Recorded (Fathom). Pull problems, don’t pitch.
  2. 15 min analysis: feed transcript to an AI agent / Claude; surface 3–7 (sometimes 5–7) off-the-shelf tools.
  3. 30 min report in Gamma (or “Claw Design” in the workshop): executive summary, priority matrix, tool recs with costs, 4-day quick start, financial impact. He has given the template away at audittemplate.ai / Kit.
  4. 30-min review call. Screen-share. Close question: “Do you want to implement these yourself, or want help?”
- **Guarantee:** money-back if he cannot find **5 hours/week** in time savings. He says he has never refunded. Average claim: 6+ hours/week for ~$40/mo in tools = “25x return on $999, every month.”
- **Stated conversion:** 40% (LinkedIn) to 50–60% (Kit + workshop) of assessments become $3–10k implementation or a concierge retainer. Assessment is now a **required** step one; he will refuse a build without it.
- **Why it converts:** cheap enough for a $1–10M owner to say yes without a committee; expensive enough that they implement; paid discovery de-risks the bigger sale; guarantee removes the “what if this is slop” objection; diagnosis-not-pitch feels like a doctor, not a vendor.
- **Sources:**

#### 1b. AI Concierge — $1,200–$2,000 / month, done-with-you retainer

- **Offer name:** AI Concierge (he also says “AI concierge consulting”).
- **Price / format:** Recurring, month-to-month in practice. Published range:
  - Kit (current “what I charge today”): **$2,000/mo**
  - LinkedIn 2026 models post: **$1,500/mo**
  - LinkedIn “$6k MRR in one week” post: closed four in a week at **$1,200 / $1,500 / $1,500 / $1,800**; he thinks the offer supports **$2,000–$2,500**
  - Vibe workshop: first yes at $1,200, then $1,500, $1,800, $2,000 — raise until more nos than yeses
- **What the buyer gets:**
  - Two 45-min Zoom calls / month, screen-share, done-with-you. Client clicks; Corey directs. Building Claude skills / implementing the assessment tools. He does **not** log into their account.
  - Unlimited Voxer (walkie-talkie) between calls. High perceived value; he reports ~1 message/month across 5 clients. 12-business-hour SLA (he actually replies in ~30 min).
  - Jotform questionnaire before call one (LinkedIn models post).
  - Call 1 is Claude onboarding + a “co-work onboarding plugin” (connectors, context files, brand voice). Zero pre-work after that except feeding the last transcript to Claude for an agenda.
  - One stakeholder per engagement (others may sit in). Client pays their own ~$20 Claude seat. Corey says his fulfillment cost is a $20 Claude sub.
- **Positioning that sells it:** “If you want me to do this *for* you, that’s $15–20k. If you want me to do it *with* you so you get the outcome *and* the learning, that’s Concierge.” Forced accountability: by month 3–4 they know how to build skills and still stay because they will not sit down without the booked hour.
- **Why it converts:** immediate ROI on call one; recurring without a project treadmill; outcome-priced so two 45-min calls at $1,500 = “$1,000/hour”; async access looks expensive and is cheap to deliver; assessment already named the work.
- **Example he gives (not a Small Bird case study — his):** fintech CEO, ~$150M company, weekly company memo 2 hours → 15 minutes via brand-voice + memo-writer skills. $2,000/mo against “hours of CEO time.”

#### 1d. Return My Time the *company* (site, Aug 2026) — different packaging

The company site has **moved upmarket and productized around knowledge bases + managed agents**. Public prices are **not** on the site. Sequence:

### 2. Marshland Software — local SMB, public price list, “concierge” as DFY infrastructure

- **Who:** Marshland Software. Phoenix / Scottsdale. Service businesses (HVAC, med-spas, law, accountants, home services, property managers, clinics, independent agencies).
- **ICP:** 3–30 employees, $500k–$10M. Explicitly *not* one-person shops, *not* 200-person companies, *not* “teach us ChatGPT,” *not* startups still finding a model.

#### 2a. AI Audit — $750, one week

- **Price / format:** **$750** fixed, one week Mon–Fri. Credits 100% toward first month of any concierge retainer. Stands alone if they walk.
- **What they get:** 2–3 hours of owner + staff interviews; written map of the **10 most-repeated workflows**; 3–5 AI opportunities with hours, cost, complexity, buy-vs-build; jargon-free written rec (including what they ruled out). No required follow-up sales call.
- **Why it converts:** “Free consults attract window shoppers; $750 attracts owners with skin in the game.” Differentiates from a 30-min pitch. Geographic ICP makes the page feel like it was written for *them*. Credit mechanic turns the audit into a coupon for the real product without cheapening it.
- **Source:** https://marshland.software/services/ai-audit

#### 2b. AI Concierge retainers — $1,500 / $3,500 / $7,500 per month

Unlike Corey’s DWY Zoom concierge, this is **they run the stack**. One bill; Marshland pays model APIs up to a cap.

| Tier | Price | Term | What they get |

| AI Essentials | **$1,500/mo** | 6-month min | 1 Claude Team or ChatGPT Business seat configured (seat billed separately); up to 3 prompt libraries / Claude Projects; 1 lightweight automation / month; monthly **60-min** live build session; Slack/SMS/email within 1 business day; quarterly 1-page briefing; API usage included |

Add-ons: custom app builds **from $40k**; voice agents quoted; document automation quoted above ~1,000 pages/mo.

- **Why it converts:** public prices, one invoice, “you never touch API keys,” 6-month floor so they can show ROI, audit credit, local proof. Month-to-month is *rejected on purpose* (“doesn’t serve anyone well here”).

## Patterns Small Bird can actually use

Twelve patterns. Each one says whether it is **generally in the market** or **copycat-too-close to Corey**, and what it would mean for a solo player-coach who already has a $300 hour and also sells content + demand.

### 1. Paid diagnosis is the door — but the *costume* is now a commodity
**Generally in the market.** Palavir $2.5/5k, Marshland $750, Ars $1k hour + $2.5k session, One Advisory $15k, AI-Native $4–7k, Corey $999, Nate’s taught $500–2.5k. Buyers have been trained to pay for a map before a build.

**Too close if Small Bird ships:** “AI Tools Assessment, $999, 45 minutes, 3–7 off-the-shelf tools, 5 hours/week or it’s free, Gamma report, review call.” That paragraph is now on other people’s sites.

**Usable version for Adam:** keep the $300 hour as the *easier* yes (this is a real differentiator — Corey and Ars both discovered that *too cheap* can devalue, but Adam already has a paid door). If a second rung is added, name it as *his* work (two or three flows, money leaks, written report + review) and do not use Corey’s nouns, guarantee wording, or tool-count.

### 2. Credit the front-door fee toward the next rung
**Generally in the market.** Marshland $750 → first retainer month. Work Smarter half of $5k briefing. iSimplifyMe 100% in 60 days. Palavir and Corey variants credit the assessment toward implementation. Return My Time’s friend-model: $999 credited into $5k/mo agents.

**Usable:** “The $300 hour credits toward a deeper assessment or a working month.” Does not require publishing those next prices until Scout/Adam lock them. Converts the hour from a dead-end product into a coupon without a 6-month lock.

### 3. Sell outcomes, not “AI”
**Generally in the market** (Corey is loudest; Nate, Marshland, LeftClick, RefinanceFlows all do it). Effectiveness / efficiency / quality. Hours, revenue leaked, appointments, proposals out the door.

**Usable:** Small Bird already talks “operating system around the work.” Tighten CTAs to one outcome per offer (“map one flow and whether it’s worth a deeper look”) rather than “AI systems & operations.”

### 4. Diagnose, then one question: “do you want help implementing?”
**Corey-specific close, now widely taught.** 40–60% said to raise a hand. Nate’s version is three prices so they choose *how*, not *whether*.

**Usable if not cloned:** the review at the end of Adam’s hour can end with a fork — “take the map and run” vs “continue into a written assessment” vs “a working month on the next problem.” Do not script Corey’s exact line on the public page.

### 5. Done-with-you is a different product from done-for-you, and should be priced like one
**Generally in the market.** Corey: DWY $1.2–2k vs “I’ll just do it” $15–20k. Marshland: DFY $1.5–7.5k with contract floors. Palavir: $25k setup then $7.5–10k/mo. Work Smarter: teach-your-people vs we-build-it.

**Usable:** Adam’s “working month, done-with-you, two 45s” is the Corey *shape*. Safe if: different name, different async channel (not Voxer-as-a-feature), month-to-month (Marshland’s 6-month floor is a contrast, not a must), and tied to *his* ICP (lean marketing/ops teams, not “any $1–10M operator”). Content + demand work can live in the same month — that is a wedge Corey does not sell.

### 6. Guarantee the *diagnosis*, or guarantee an *operating number* — never a vague ROI
**Generally in the market, two flavors.**
- Diagnosis guarantees: Corey / AI Initiator “5 hours/week or refund”; Palavir “refund if nothing beats the current process”; ClearForge “3+ measurable opportunities.”
- Operating guarantees: RefinanceFlows 10 appointments or refund; Maker School first client in 90 days; AI-Native production by week 7 or 50% back.

**Too close:** “5 hours a week or you don’t pay,” especially next to a $999 assessment.

**Usable:** if Adam wants a guarantee later, Palavir’s “if nothing is worth automating, the fee comes back” is the cleaner, less-cloned version. Or no public guarantee on the $300 hour — the low price already de-risks it.

### 7. Put the prices on the page
**Generally in the market among operators who actually want inbound.** Marshland, Palavir, One Advisory, Ars, Prestyj, Work Smarter’s $5k door, LeftClick’s “starts at $5k/$10k.” Hidden-price alignment calls are the education-funnel pattern (Ottley), not the consultancy pattern.

**Usable:** keep $300 public. If Scout locks an assessment / working-month number, put it on the page. If not locked, Adam’s current copy (“priced when we know the work”) is honest and better than a fake $999.

### 8. Niche or geography beats “AI for any small business”
**Generally in the market.** Marshland = Phoenix service businesses + Housecall Pro. Palavir = messy regulated data in SE Michigan + chambers. RefinanceFlows = aged refi lists. Corey *teaches* go-broad-then-niche but his examples are e-com / Amazon / wedding venue / business broker / insurance marketing director. Vibe marketing / Boring is *marketers*. LeftClick is B2B growth systems.

**Usable:** Small Bird’s real niche is already on the page — lean teams that need **marketing and AI operations without an agency layer**. That is closer to Dickerson’s “one marketer = five” than to Corey’s plumber-with-a-calendar. Lean into content, demand, intake, and the system around them. ### 9. A free 15–30 min qualify is fine *above* a paid hour — it is not a substitute
**Generally in the market.** Ars 15 min → $1k hour. LeftClick 30 min → $10–50k project. Return My Time 30 min → paid assessment. Palavir fit call → $2.5k audit. Corey now uses a qualifying form that rejects education-seekers.

**Usable:** Adam already skipped the free call and charges $300. That is rarer and more honest. Do not add a free “strategy call” that turns the hour into a sales pitch. If anything, add a *written* free scorecard (Palavir style) that still leaves the $300 as the first human work.

### 10. Workshops / briefings / meetups are a second door, especially for content+demand
**Generally in the market.** Work Smarter $5k exec briefing; Ars lunch-and-learn / half-day / full-day; Palavir free chamber workshops; Corey free city meetup as lead-gen; Nate / Dickerson live sessions inside paid communities.

**Usable for Small Bird without new prices:** an in-person or private-team working session (AI + messaging + intake) is on-brand and *not* a Corey clone. Quote it; don’t publish a number Adam hasn’t locked. Local meetup as trust, not as the product.

### 11. One workflow in production beats a transformation roadmap
**Generally in the market.** One Advisory “one loop.” Palavir “one Claude pipeline in 10 days.” Nate “first project $2.5–10k.” Corey “don’t start at offer 7.” AI-Native phases the 12-month roadmap so each workflow is its own fixed price.

**Usable:** the page already says the hour maps *one flow*. The next paid rung should stay “the next problem,” not “your 12-month AI transformation.” If a build SKU ever appears, one named system (intake, follow-up, content engine) with a fixed scope — not a menu of 3–7 tools.

### 12. Recurring revenue is either accountability (DWY) or operations (DFY) — pick one per SKU
**Generally in the market.** Corey keeps people because he is a forcing function. Marshland keeps people because they *run* the workflows and pay the APIs. Work Smarter keeps people because someone senior owns adoption for the board.

**Usable:** Adam’s “ongoing partnership / player-coach” is the first kind. Do not also promise “we host and manage your agents” unless there is a technical partner (Corey’s own split: he sells concierge alone; KB/agents with Nick). A solo DWY month that also covers content and demand is a coherent SKU. A solo “managed AI employee” is not, yet.
